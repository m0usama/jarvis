Now for your learning moment on data modelling: star schemas and dbt.

When organisations collect operational data, it usually lives in transactional formats designed for fast row-by-row updates, often normalising information across dozens of tables to avoid duplication. While this is ideal for recording single print orders or stock updates, querying it for reporting requires massive, slow joins across fragmented relational schemas.

A star schema simplifies this by splitting data into two distinct categories: fact tables and dimension tables. The fact table sits in the centre, containing quantitative, numerical measurements of business events, like an order quantity, total cost, or production duration, accompanied by foreign keys. Surrounding it like the points of a star are dimension tables, which contain the descriptive context, such as customer details, machine specifications, or calendar dates. 

Consider a print business workflow. A transactional database might store jobs, customer profiles, address books, and substrate inventories across four separate tables. In a star schema, you build a single central fact table, fact print jobs, where each row represents a completed job with metrics like sheet count, print duration, and revenue. You then link this to dimension tables: dim customer, containing business name and account tier; dim machine, containing press type and feeder capacity; and dim date. An analytics query calculating total sheets printed by account tier requires only a single join between the fact table and the customer dimension, executing in a fraction of the time.

This is where dbt, or data build tool, enters modern engineering. Rather than writing brittle scheduled extraction scripts that manually push transformed tables around, dbt lets you write simple declarative select statements in version-controlled SQL files. You define the logic that transforms raw, ingested landing tables into staging views, and finally into production-ready fact and dimension tables. Dbt automatically infers the dependency graph, runs tests on primary keys, and compiles documentation. In real data engineering, combining dbt with a clean dimensional star schema turns fragile transactional dumps into reliable, audit-ready data models that analysts and downstream automations can depend on without breaking.
