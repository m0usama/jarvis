This brings us to today's learning moment: embeddings and vector databases. An embedding is simply a way of translating text into a list of numbers, known as a vector, such that pieces of text with similar meanings end up close to each other in mathematical space. Instead of searching for literal matching words like a standard SQL query, you convert a user question into a vector and measure which stored text vectors are closest to it.

Imagine your product catalogue restructuring work. If a customer searches for protective outdoor signs, traditional keyword search might miss a product titled durable weather-resistant vinyl banner because none of the words match. With embeddings, a model translates both phrases into numerical vectors. Because the conceptual meaning is similar, their vectors point in nearly the exact same direction. A vector database is a specialised storage engine designed to hold millions of these vectors and run fast similarity searches to find the nearest neighbours. In professional AI engineering, this is the foundational retrieval step that provides relevant business context to an LLM before it generates a response.
